## The mark

**Governing idea:** Intellectual Frontiers = natural frontiers. A mountain
range, coastline, high pass, or other difficult terrain is the idea this
brand returns to. The landscape represents the frontier. The wordmark
represents the thinking. The space between them represents discovery. This
joins the parent company and the natural-frontier illustration language used
by Intellectual Frontiers Press into one recognizable family.

**What it is:** a frontier landscape, not an abstraction. A hand-drawn
mountain, lake, and forest scene sits to the left of a two-line Intellectual
Frontiers wordmark in Deep Ink. The landscape enters from the left and
overlaps the type slightly. That overlap is deliberate. It is one full
lockup, in a light-surface version and a dark-surface version.

| Part | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Landscape | Reality as it is encountered: complex, uneven, partly known, and often difficult to navigate. |
| Path and pass | Inquiry moving toward something not yet fully understood, without pretending the answer is already known. |
| Open horizon | Possibility, unanswered questions, and the room required for judgment. |
| Wordmark | Precise thinking that turns field observation into knowledge that can be tested, communicated, and used. |

**Progression:** Reality → Exploration → Observation → Understanding. Serious
thinking begins in the real world, moves through difficult terrain, and
becomes understanding.

**Why this direction:** Intellectual means inquiry, judgment, ideas,
reasoning, and accumulated knowledge. Frontiers means exploration, distance,
difficult terrain, and movement toward what is not yet understood. The logo
combines those ideas rather than illustrating either one alone. It should
feel like an expedition sketch meeting modern technical publishing:
grounded, durable, calm, curious, and precise. It should not feel
whimsical, boastful, or stiff. The landscape belongs to the same visual
family as the Press cover library; the logo uses restrained natural color so
it stays finished and legible at small sizes, while Press cover
illustrations remain monochrome.

**Preserve:**
- The two-line Intellectual Frontiers wordmark.
- The landscape primarily on the left.
- The slight overlap between landscape and typography.
- Strong white space and clear readability.
- Restrained natural color and an engraved, field-sketch character.

**Do not add or imitate:**
- Sun icons, clouds, light bulbs, brains, books, neural networks, circuit
  imagery, generic AI symbols, compass icons, arrows, badges, circular
  mountain emblems, and decorative slogans.
- Outdoor apparel, national park, tourism, ski resort, or generic
  venture-capital mountain styling.

**Conceptual test** for any new use or size:
1. Does this communicate intellectual inquiry and frontier exploration
   together?
2. Does it feel like a serious organization exploring difficult ideas rather
   than an outdoor recreation company?
3. Does it remain recognizable and legible at the intended size?
4. Does the landscape support the wordmark rather than overpower it?

## Approved artwork

The supplied light-surface and dark-surface PNG files are the approved
lockups. Each has real transparency. Use the version made for its background
and the size made for the job rather than enlarging a smaller export.
**No vector source exists yet.** Each set holds six scaled exports of one
master (they are not independently simplified redraws), all named
`if-logo-<width>x<height>-2026-Sept.png` (light) or
`if-logo-dark-<width>x<height>-2026-Sept.png` (dark):

| Size (px) | Use |
| --- | --- |
| 150 × 36 | Favicons, small interface chrome, and extreme reduction |
| 299 × 72 | Email signatures and compact web headers (dark set: superfooters, email signatures, and compact web placements) |
| 395 × 100 | Standard web headers and social profiles |
| 535 × 148 | Slide decks and mid-size print material (dark set: slide decks and mid-size presentation material) |
| 672 × 189 | Larger print material and letterhead (dark set: larger presentation and display use) |
| 1229 × 362 | Print-resolution display use and master artwork |

These files are held by the website, not in this repository (`assets/logos/`
holds only the superseded v1.3 artwork; see the last section).

**Responsive system:** both background variants use the same proportions and
size family. Larger versions may carry mountain linework, ridges, trees,
shoreline, reflections, and engraving texture. At smaller sizes, legibility
matters more than detail. A future small-size redraw may simplify secondary
ridges, trees, water detail, and cross-hatching while preserving the main
silhouette and wordmark. The 150 × 36 files remain legible; true responsive
redraws are work for a later refresh.

## Logo use

- Use the supplied full lockup. Never recreate, redraw, recolor, regenerate,
  or type the wordmark beside another image.
- No approved standalone icon exists. Do not crop the mountain scene from
  the lockup.
- Keep clear space around the full lockup at least equal to the cap-height
  of the wordmark's I on all four sides.
- Do not place the lockup below 100 pixels wide in digital work or one inch
  wide in print.
- Use the standard lockup on white or very light backgrounds and the
  supplied dark-mode lockup on dark backgrounds.
- Keep the native proportions. Never stretch, distort, skew, rotate,
  outline, shadow, glow, or add a background panel.
- Do not combine the logo with a spiral, dot pattern, or another abstract
  geometric mark.
- Never enlarge an export beyond its native pixel dimensions.
- Keep a transparent background unless a specific approved use requires
  otherwise.

Do not rename the company "What IF?" or add a question mark to the mark, and
do not place taglines, program names, or legal suffixes inside the logo
(carried over from v1.3; the current reference does not restate them).

## Naming architecture

| Level | Naming | Rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Corporate | Intellectual Frontiers | Use the full lockup without a descriptor. |
| Operating unit | Intellectual Frontiers Capital / Research & IP / Press / Studios / Network | Write the unit name after "Intellectual Frontiers" in text. There is no unit lockup (the v1.3 rule of setting the unit name on line two of a logo in its assigned color is superseded). |
| Program or product | Descriptive name endorsed by the relevant unit | Keep the program name outside the logo. Use "from" or "by" language in copy. |
| Legal entity | Full legal name where required | Use LLC and other legal suffixes in contracts and notices, never inside the logo. |

**Extension rules:**
- Place new work under Corporate, Capital, Research & IP, Press, Studios, or
  Network before creating another brand.
- Use the existing unit color when an extension belongs to a unit.
- Do not create a separate symbol for a program.
- A fifth peer unit, Network, is now permanent. The whole architecture is
  under review before another unit or color is added.

**Naming rules:**
- Use the full name on first external reference: Intellectual Frontiers
  Capital, Research & IP, Press, Studios, or Network.
- Use Capital, IP, Press, Studios, or Network alone only after context is
  established. ("Research & IP" is the unit's short form; "IP" alone is still
  used in running text.)
- Do not recreate the logo with typed text. Use the supplied lockup.

## Typography

| Role | Face | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Primary sans | Inter | Websites, presentations, reports, marketing, data, navigation, and operating documents. |
| Office fallback | Arial | Word, PowerPoint, email, and systems where Inter is unavailable. |
| Editorial serif | Charter | Long-form Press publications, books, essays, and selective editorial display use. |
| Serif fallback | Georgia | Long-form material where Charter is unavailable. |

| Element | Spec |
| --- | --- |
| Display title | Inter 700, 28–44 pt, Deep Ink |
| Section heading | Inter 700, 18–24 pt, Deep Ink |
| Subheading | Inter 600, 12–16 pt, unit color or Deep Ink |
| Body | Inter 400, 10–12 pt, Deep Ink |
| Caption or metadata | Inter 500, 8–10 pt, muted gray |
| Long-form editorial | Charter 400, 10.5–12 pt, Deep Ink |

The current reference adds one rule: Inter is the wordmark and cover family.
Use Bold for the name and primary titles, Regular or Medium for supporting
text, and SemiBold for names and emphasis. Do not recreate the logo with live
type; the type rule applies only when the company name appears in ordinary
text.

## Voice

| Principle | Write | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Evidence-led | State the evidence, assumption, and decision. | Unsupported claims and narrative certainty. |
| Practical | Name the smallest useful experiment and the next decision. | Large plans without a testable wedge. |
| Skeptical | Say what would prove the claim wrong. | Advocacy dressed as analysis. |
| Plain | Use specific nouns, active verbs, and short sentences. | Consulting jargon, AI hype, and promotional adjectives. |

This applies to any AI-assisted drafting done in this repository or on
Intellectual Frontiers' behalf, not only human-written copy.

The four principles above are the summary. The full writing guide —
first-person vs. corporate point of view, banned vocabulary, the audit
pass, and the ASD-STE100 switch for procedures — is
[`context/writing-guide.md`](writing-guide.md).

**Questions any brand-facing material should be able to answer:**
1. What unusual advantage is already present?
2. Why does this person or organization have it?
3. Who cares enough to spend a scarce resource?
4. What is the smallest experiment that can prove or disprove value?
5. What makes the advantage difficult to copy?
6. If successful, how does each use strengthen the next one?

## Application and control

- Keep each dated export set so older material can be traced. When the logo
  itself changes, update the canonical source first, then propagate it to
  websites, covers, documents, slides, email signatures, and other surfaces.
  Never work in the opposite direction.
- When a new size is needed: start from the approved 1229 × 362 master;
  preserve the wordmark's proportions and placement; simplify landscape
  detail only when legibility requires it; preserve the overall silhouette
  and the relationship between image and wordmark; keep a transparent
  background unless a specific approved use requires otherwise.
- The wordmark represents the thinking. The landscape represents the
  frontier. The logo works because neither tries to do both jobs.

### Asset handling

- Use the supplied PNG exports at or below their native pixel dimensions.
  There is no SVG source yet; when one exists, SVG becomes the source format
  and PNG is exported from it. (The v1.3 rule "SVG is the source format" is
  superseded for now.)
- Do not use screenshots or copied web images.
- Preserve transparent backgrounds.
- Do not edit master artwork inside presentation, social media, or page
  layout tools.

### Release checklist

- [ ] Correct light or dark lockup selected, at a size made for the job.
- [ ] Clear space and minimum size preserved.
- [ ] Color and background rules followed.
- [ ] Name written correctly and legal suffix used only where required.
- [ ] Claims supported by evidence; AI described as an amplifier, not the
      advantage.
- [ ] No new logo, color, tagline, or extension created without owner
      approval.

## Superseded: the v1.3 Five-Logo System

> **Retired 2026-09-18.** Everything below describes the identity before the
> natural-frontier landscape lockup. It is kept as history and so that
> nothing in `assets/logos/` is orphaned without an explanation. Do not use
> it for new work. The website no longer carries any of it: its roadmap
> records "Remove active four-arm and unit-lockup identity claims."

**The core graphic:** four spiral arms of nine dots each, one arm per
operating unit (IP, Capital, Studios, Press). The overall company had no
separate arm or color; all four arms together were Intellectual Frontiers.
Network had a color for documents and screens but no arm on the graphic.
Geometry, spacing, perspective, dot positions, and arm colors were fixed.

**Color rules (v1.3, retired with the graphic):**
- Keep the core graphic's four colors in the approved arm assignment.
- On dark fields, use the full-color graphic only when every arm remains
  distinct.
- For one-color production, use all Deep Ink on light backgrounds or all
  white on dark backgrounds.

**Naming (v1.3, retired):** "Operating unit | Intellectual Frontiers Capital
/ IP / Press / Studios / Network | Append the unit name after Frontiers on
line two in its assigned color." Extension rules: do not add another arm or
recolor the corporate graphic; a fifth peer unit, Network, is permanent and
the whole architecture is under review before another arm or color is added
to the graphic.

**Clear space (v1.3, retired):**

| Asset | Space | Min (digital) | Min (print) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Core graphic | One largest-dot diameter on every side | 24 px | 0.33 in |
| Corporate lockup | One largest-dot diameter around the full lockup | 140 px wide | 1.25 in wide |
| Unit lockup | One largest-dot diameter around the full lockup | 160 px wide | 1.4 in wide |

**Do not (v1.3, retired):** rotate further, flatten, stretch, crop, outline,
bevel, animate, or redraw the mark; change dot sizes, positions, arm
assignments, or color order; add gradients, transparency, lighting, or
effects beyond the approved shallow depth and shadow; move the unit name to
another line or detach it from the parent name; use the retired single-line
lockup; place taglines, program names, legal suffixes, or "What IF?" inside
the logo.
